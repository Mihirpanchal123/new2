# Pokémon Arena

Pokémon battles in Retro 2D pixel art or 3D with 491 Pokémon from all nine generations (full evolution lines, starters of every generation, every legendary and mythical box/trio Pokémon, and every Pokémon that has a Mega) and 96 Mega Evolutions, real stats, level-up movesets plus classic TM / tutor moves, 440 usable moves and the official damage formula.

- **Real 3D Pokémon:** animated 3D models for the original 111 forms (Pokémon added later appear as their animated sprite in 3D until a model is downloaded) with a procedural rig — breathing, walking, lunging, recoiling, roaring, fainting. Models with full game animations use them.
- **Trainers:** a 3D trainer per player (outfits: Red, Blue, Leaf, May, Dawn, Ethan; custom names). They throw the Poké Ball, point to command, raise their Key Stone for Mega Evolution, cheer and despair.
- **3D move effects:** GPU particles, energy beams, lightning, shockwaves, debris, ground scorch/crack/frost marks, dynamic lights and bloom. Every move has an effect styled by its type (beam, stream, orb, bolt, sky strike, quake, rock slide, slash, bite, punch, buff, heal, drain, powder, shield...).
- **Broadcast camera:** high-angle view with close-ups on attacks, throws, Mega Evolutions and faints.
- **Broadcast presentation:** original synthesized music that intensifies in pinch moments, move sound effects, live crowd, anime-style move banners, floating damage numbers, CRITICAL / SUPER EFFECTIVE pop-ups and HP bars over each Pokémon.
- **Cinematic:** intro with drone fly-over, title card, trainer walk-outs with spotlights, team reveal, "who will win?" prompt and 3-2-1 countdown (click or Space skips it); attacker close-ups, camera follows projectiles, slow motion on crits/KOs, low-angle Mega Evolution, a replay of the battle-winning blow and a victory orbit.
- **Series & reach:** 🏆 8-trainer tournaments with 12 original CPU trainers (bracket screens, champion ceremony, resumable), 📊 saved records / Hall of Fame, an automatic highlights reel after each battle (saved as its own video while recording), 🖼 auto thumbnails, and 📱 Shorts mode (9:16 — the recording is cropped to the vertical frame in Chrome/Edge).
- **6 arenas:** Pokémon Stadium, Viridian Forest, Mt. Chimney Crater, Sunny Beach, Ice Path Cavern and Lumiose Night Arena.
- **👾 Retro 2D mode** (switch at the top of the team builder): top-down pixel-art battles in real time. Both Pokémon move around and attack on their own cooldowns, so beams can meet mid-field (**CLASH!**, the stronger beam pushes through). Animated sprites, pixel trainers, pixel move effects (fire pillars, beams, lightning, quakes, rock slides, slashes...), scorch marks that build up, a cheering crowd and a broadcast HUD: portrait, type and stat-change chips (ATK+2, DEF-1), HP bar with number, party balls, move-name pills, timer, damage numbers, SUPER EFFECTIVE / CRITICAL text and "X FAINTED!" banners. 5 places: Prism Plaza (city plaza at night with string lights), Verdant Park, Sunset Beach, Frost Village and Magma Quarry (the Arena choice picks the matching one). Same stats, damage formula, type chart, AI, Megas, records, tournaments, recording and Shorts mode as 3D. Human players click a move (or press 1–4) when their Pokémon is ready, or a party button to switch.
  - *Movement:* Pokémon accelerate and ease to a stop, circle each other at a range that suits their next move (physical attackers close in first), hop-walk, lean into runs, flip when turning, squash and stretch on hits, wind up before lunges and leave afterimages when dashing. Big hits launch the target; fainted Pokémon are recalled in a red beam.
  - *Attack effects:* soft glow and ground lighting, scene dimming for big moves, camera punch-ins and hit-stop on heavy hits. Each type has its own projectile, stream, beam and impact (fireballs and flame tongues, water splashes, branching lightning, ice crystals, earth chunks...). Every physical move has its own animation (purple Dragon Claw talons, Leaf Blade, X-Scissor, elemental fangs and fists, tail swipes, wing strikes...), and many signature moves are animated individually: Solar Beam, Psychic (lift and slam), Earthquake cracks, Rock Slide boulders, Stone Edge spires, Vine Whip, Leaf Storm, Moonblast, Future Sight, Close Combat, Body Slam and more.
  - *Two-turn & recharge moves:* Solar Beam gathers sunlight before firing; Fly rises out of sight (only its shadow glides over) and dives; Dig burrows, races underground as a moving mound and erupts under the target; Hyper Beam / Giga Impact need a recharge. While flying or underground a Pokémon can only be hit by the moves that reach it (Thunder/Hurricane/Gust/Twister vs Fly, Earthquake vs Dig, some at double damage). They work the same way in 3D (as two-turn moves) and are learnable as classic TMs (Solar Beam: Grass types, Fly: Flying types, Dig: Ground/Rock types, Hyper Beam: fully evolved Pokémon).
  - *Large arena (world vs view):* the battlefield is about 5x the area of the original one-screen field (2.24x on each axis in landscape; 1.7x across and 2.9x in depth in 9:16), with the scenery band, fenced sides, procedural props and the stands built around it at its own scale (procedural pixel art, nothing stretched). World size, spawn spacing, fighting distance, roaming radius and every camera limit are in the ARENA config at the top of retro.js. Pokémon use the space: repositioning runs stay within reach of the foe, duels drift toward parts of the field they have not used yet, they cross to the far side of the foe, sprint long distances, and physical moves start with a real run-up when the target is far away. Damage numbers and labels are world-anchored; the HUD stays in screen space.
  - *Group camera:* the base shot fits a box around every active Pokémon (sprite, name tag, shadow) into the part of the screen not covered by the HUD (and the Reels caption in 9:16), and also zooms by how far apart they are: close duels get a close camera, separation pulls it back. Dead zones on zoom and position stop it breathing while they circle; zooming out to keep everyone in frame always wins over smoothing and over the minimum-zoom limit, and the target is clamped to the world edges. Event shots (focus, wind-up, dash tracking, impact, finishing knockout) lean and push in on top of the group framing but are capped so no active Pokémon leaves the frame.
  - *Camera director:* a base framing keeps every Pokémon and projectile in view; battle events then request prioritised shots on top of it: lean toward whoever acts, a push-in on big wind-ups, tracking a dash, a punch-in on each landed hit sized by the damage actually dealt, and a cinematic finish on the deciding knockout. The highest-priority shot wins (so the camera never ping-pongs between two events), every transition is eased by a critically damped spring, shots expire back to the overview on their own, and shake is smooth rather than random per frame. Camera offsets and sprite anchors snap to whole device pixels so pixel art does not shimmer. The HUD and banners stay fixed while damage numbers move with the action.
  - *Dash strikes* (Quick Attack, Extreme Speed, Aqua Jet, Accelerock...): anticipation (crouch, lean back, eye glint), an accelerating curved dash with afterimages and the camera tracking it, contact with hit-stop and a damage-sized punch-in, the target's recoil, follow-through past the target with a skid, and a hop back to range. A miss, Protect or an immunity shows a whiff instead of an impact (for every physical move), and damage is still applied once by the battle code.
  - *Movement personalities:* from base Speed and on-screen size: fast Pokémon snap into motion, heavy ones are slow to start and stop with weighty footfalls (dust and a faint tremor), small ones take short quick hops, and flyers lift as they pick up speed.
  - *Whole-arena movement:* each run is a tactic (sweep around the foe, cross to its other side, reposition far away, retreat to a corner when hurt, press in for a physical hit). Physical attackers hit and run, long runs become sprints with dust, and Pokémon pause briefly between runs, so they swap sides and use the whole field.
  - *Arena damage:* big hits blast craters (dark pit, raised rim, loose cobbles and pebbles, smoke). Fire leaves burning patches that become scorch marks, Water leaves puddles that splash when walked through, Ice freezes the ground, Poison leaves bubbling pools, Electric leaves crackling burns, Lava Plume leaves a lava pool that cools to rock, Earthquake rips fissures across the field, and Rock Slide / Stone Edge leave boulders. The damage builds up over the battle and resets for the next one.
  - *Stats drive the fight:* how often a Pokémon attacks and how snappy its wind-ups, lunges and charges are come from its real Speed (with stages and paralysis), so fast Pokémon act first and more often. Impact size, screen shake and stagger follow the actual damage dealt as a share of max HP. Knockback compares the attacker's Attack/Sp. Atk with the defender's Defense/Sp. Def: sturdy Pokémon dig in and barely move, frail ones get sent flying. Protect reads, switching and replacement picks use the official damage formula both ways plus who is faster.
  - *Strategy:* trainers recall a Pokémon stuck in a bad type matchup and send in a counter ("Come back!" → red recall beam → "Go!"; at most 3 switches per side, 12 s apart). Attacks in flight whiff when their target leaves. A Pokémon that knows Protect/Detect reads big or super-effective attacks and blocks them in time, and CPUs don't waste attacks into a Protect. Human players can switch with the party buttons under their moves.
  - *Defence & timing:* a short ready crouch telegraphs every attack; heavy hits and critical hits stagger the target (dizzy stars on crits) and make it back off; resisted hits flash a shield, immune Pokémon shrug; fast Pokémon side-hop between attacks; priority moves recover faster. Trainers shout some commands and react when their Pokémon is hit hard.
  - *Physical moves* play in three beats (wind-up, strike, follow-through with a brief impact freeze): Bite/Crunch jaws snap shut with a head-shake, Double Kick is a jump kick plus a spinning kick, tails whip through the target, Quick Attack blurs in with afterimages. Status moves have body language too (Growl/Roar sound waves, Tail Whip wag, Leer glare, Taunt anger mark, Protect dome, Hypnosis spiral).
  - *Mega Evolution:* the Key Stone shines, a rainbow helix links trainer and Pokémon, a sphere of light gathers energy and shatters, and the new form appears with the Mega emblem. It owns the battle: it waits until no attack is in motion (nobody starts a new one meanwhile), then every other Pokémon stops, turns to watch and holds its moves, status damage and the clock pause, and the fight resumes once the new form is revealed.

- **🧊 2.5D Pokémon (beta, Retro 2D):** a few Pokémon (Bulbasaur, Grovyle, Mewtwo, Dragonite) have sprite sheets pre-rendered from their 3D models with the arena's light and camera, outlined and palette-reduced to sit with the pixel art; they play real idle / run / attack / hurt / faint animations, anchored at the feet. Turn off with the setting. Everything else, and Mega forms, use the normal sprites. See `sprites25d/README.md`; new sheets are made with `tools/bake-25d.mjs`.
- **🎲 Live 3D Pokémon (prototype, Retro 2D):** Pokémon with a model in `models3d/` (currently Mewtwo) are rendered from their real animated 3D model every frame (three.js from `lib/`, loaded only when needed), reduced to pixel art with a palette, outline and their own cast shadow, and drawn in the 2D battle like any sprite. The model turns in 3D, and its idle, run, attack, special, hit, stagger → recovery, faint, roar and victory animations follow the battle events. It takes precedence over the 2.5D sheet. Turn off with the setting. See `models3d/README.md`.
- **👑 Battle Royale & 👥 Team Brawl** (Retro 2D, pick the format next to the 3D / Retro switch): up to 8 Pokémon on the field at once. *Battle Royale* is a free-for-all (2–8 entrants, last one standing wins); *Team Brawl* puts both whole teams on the field together (1–4 per side). Each Pokémon picks its own target (close, weakened or one it hits hard), gets a name tag and HP bar over its head, and the HUD shows a mini card per Pokémon plus a knockout feed. Intro card, sudden death, the final-blow replay and the MVP end card all work here too. To keep a busy field readable: Pokémon pair off into duels (they leave alone a Pokémon that is already under attack; in Team Brawl each one has a lane opponent) and keep away from other duels; at most two attacks play at once, and a big move (Mega Evolution, the deciding blow, a sure knockout or a 120+ power move, at most every 4 s) takes the stage alone while the camera zooms onto that duel and everyone else fades back; minor texts, move names and screen shake/flashes of ordinary hits are toned down; Team Brawl draws a team-coloured ring under each Pokémon and lock-on brackets mark every new target. Multi battles are CPU only and are not added to the records.

## Run it
Double-click **`start.bat`**. It starts a tiny local server and opens http://localhost:8080 in Chrome/Edge.
The game must run through that server (the 3D engine loads as modules); opening `index.html` directly shows a reminder.

The game starts in light **Retro 2D** mode: only the 2D engine loads (no three.js, no WebGL, about 2 MB of memory). The 3D engine (three.js, renderer, 3D arena and trainers) loads the first time you pick **🎮 3D**, or at startup if 3D was your last choice; the 3D models only download when a 3D battle starts.

## Play
1. **Team builder:** browse the Pokédex by generation tab (All, Gen 1–9) or search, then click Pokémon to add them to the highlighted player. On a phone the settings fold behind **⚙ Settings**. Set level, Mega Stone, 4 moves, or evolve/de-evolve each slot.
   Evolved forms can't go below their real evolution level (Charizard ≥ 36). Pick Human or CPU for each player.
2. **Battle:** choose a move, or switch. If your Pokémon holds a Mega Stone, toggle **Mega Evolve** before picking a move (once per battle).
3. **Auto-play:** tick "Auto-play next random battle" for endless CPU vs CPU battles.

## Record for upload
Click **⏺ Record** (or press **R**), then pick **this tab** and tick "share tab audio" for the cries.
Every battle is saved as its own video (`pokemon-battle-<date>.mp4`) in your Downloads folder. Press R again to stop.
Combine with auto-play + Turbo speed to record lots of battles hands-free.

## Add more Pokémon
Add an evolution chain's first species to `ROOTS` in `tools/build-data.mjs`, then run:
```
node tools/build-data.mjs
node tools/build-models.mjs
```
Data, sprites and cries come from PokéAPI (https://pokeapi.co).

## Rebuild 3D models / libraries
```
node tools/build-models.mjs
```
Downloads the .glb models for every species in data.js from the Pokémon 3D API asset repo
(https://github.com/Pokemon-3D-api/assets — fan project, non-commercial) and three.js r170 into `lib/`.
`tools/model-preview.html` (open via the server) shows models side by side.

The Retro 2D font is Tiny5 (SIL Open Font License, Google Fonts) in `lib/fonts/`.

## Not implemented (yet)
Abilities, held items other than Mega Stones, weather/terrain, and a few rare moves (Sky Attack, Dive, Phantom Force, Explosion…). Moves the engine can't run are hidden from move lists.
