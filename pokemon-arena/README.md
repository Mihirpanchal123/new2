# Pokémon Arena

Real-time Pokémon battles in Retro 2D pixel art with 481 Pokémon from all nine generations (full evolution lines, starters of every generation, every legendary and mythical box/trio Pokémon) and 63 Mega Evolutions, real stats, level-up movesets plus classic TM / tutor moves, 440 usable moves and the official damage formula.

- **Series & reach:** 🏆 8-trainer tournaments with 12 original CPU trainers (bracket screens, champion ceremony, resumable), 📊 saved records / Hall of Fame, 🖼 auto thumbnails, and 📱 Shorts mode (9:16 — the recording is cropped to the vertical frame in Chrome/Edge).
- **Presentation:** original synthesized music that intensifies in pinch moments, move sound effects and cries, a live crowd, a title card and countdown before each battle, slow motion and a replay of the battle-winning blow, an MVP end card.
- **👾 Retro 2D battles:** top-down pixel-art battles in real time. Both Pokémon move around and attack on their own cooldowns, so beams can meet mid-field (**CLASH!**, the stronger beam pushes through). Animated sprites, pixel trainers, pixel move effects (fire pillars, beams, lightning, quakes, rock slides, slashes...), scorch marks that build up, a cheering crowd and a broadcast HUD: portrait, type and stat-change chips (ATK+2, DEF-1), HP bar with number, party balls, move-name pills, timer, damage numbers, SUPER EFFECTIVE / CRITICAL text and "X FAINTED!" banners. 5 places: Prism Plaza (city plaza at night with string lights), Verdant Park, Sunset Beach, Frost Village and Magma Quarry (the Arena choice picks the matching one). Real stats, the official damage formula and type chart, the AI, Megas, records, tournaments, recording and Shorts mode all work here. Human players get a control panel: four big move cards (type, category and power, PP, and a SUPER! / WEAK / NO EFFECT badge against the foe), a charge bar that says when the next move goes off (tap a move early and it is marked NEXT and fires as soon as the bar fills), and the bench with HP bars to switch. Click or tap a card, or press 1–4.
  - *Movement:* Pokémon accelerate and ease to a stop, circle each other at a range that suits their next move (physical attackers close in first), hop-walk, lean into runs, flip when turning, squash and stretch on hits, wind up before lunges and leave afterimages when dashing. Big hits launch the target; fainted Pokémon are recalled in a red beam.
  - *Attack effects:* soft glow and ground lighting, scene dimming for big moves, camera punch-ins and hit-stop on heavy hits. Each type has its own projectile, stream, beam and impact (fireballs and flame tongues, water splashes, branching lightning, ice crystals, earth chunks...). Every physical move has its own animation (purple Dragon Claw talons, Leaf Blade, X-Scissor, elemental fangs and fists, tail swipes, wing strikes...), and many signature moves are animated individually: Solar Beam, Psychic (lift and slam), Earthquake cracks, Rock Slide boulders, Stone Edge spires, Vine Whip, Leaf Storm, Moonblast, Future Sight, Close Combat, Body Slam and more.
  - *Anime fight layer* (none of it changes damage): **rhythm** - a stand-off before big moves (both hold still, wind crosses between them, then the burst), a **cut-in** banner for ultimate moves (Flare Blitz, Close Combat, Hydro Cannon, Wave Crash, Hyper Beam...), and **rush exchanges** where both meet mid-field, clash and trade blows at speed, flash-stepping around each other until the attacker's blow breaks through; **speed** - fast Pokémon **flash-step** out of attacks (leaving an afterimage that takes the hit) and counter, heavy blows **launch** the target so the attacker can follow up and spike it into a crater, very fast motion smears the sprite and draws **speed lines**; **impact** - a one-beat high-contrast **impact frame** with focus lines on the heaviest hits (Settings: ⚡ Impact flashes), a camera **kick** in the direction of every blow; **the arena** - knockback ploughs a **trench**, a Pokémon knocked into the side **fence breaks** it (into the stands, the crowd gasps), big impacts send a **shockwave** across the ground that cracks it and lifts slabs, dust hangs in the air, **rubble** builds up over the battle, and the deciding knockout (if the blow is big enough) **launches** the loser across the arena into the fence.
  - *Staged signature moves* that change the arena: **Flamethrower** sweeps a roaring stream across a wide fan, flames rolling along the ground, which stays scorched and keeps burning; **Flare Blitz** bursts into flame, charges as a fireball that scorches a burning trail, explodes on the target (crater, ring of fire, smoke column) and is thrown back by its own blast; **Psychic** spreads a rippling field over the ground around the target, tears chunks of arena loose to float while the target is lifted and wrung, then slams everything down; **Ancient Power** makes rocks burst out of craters around the user, rise glowing and gather above it, then hurls them one by one, the big one last (leaving a boulder); **Aura Sphere** draws aura in from all around to grow a sphere between the user's hands, then fires it big with a swirling trail and a blue blast; **Close Combat** is a 9-blow flurry of jabs and kicks finished by a launching uppercut; **Hydro Cannon** spirals water in from the ground into a growing sphere, fires a beam twice as thick with heavy recoil and floods the ground; **Wave Crash** raises a tidal wave that rolls across the whole arena with the user riding its crest into the target, and leaves the arena soaked.
  - *Two-turn & recharge moves:* Solar Beam gathers sunlight before firing; Fly rises out of sight (only its shadow glides over) and dives; Dig burrows, races underground as a moving mound and erupts under the target; Hyper Beam / Giga Impact need a recharge. While flying or underground a Pokémon can only be hit by the moves that reach it (Thunder/Hurricane/Gust/Twister vs Fly, Earthquake vs Dig, some at double damage). They are learnable as classic TMs (Solar Beam: Grass types, Fly: Flying types, Dig: Ground/Rock types, Hyper Beam: fully evolved Pokémon).
  - *Large arena (world vs view):* the battlefield is about 5x the area of the original one-screen field (2.24x on each axis in landscape; 1.7x across and 2.9x in depth in 9:16), with the scenery band, fenced sides, procedural props and the stands built around it at its own scale (procedural pixel art, nothing stretched). World size, spawn spacing, fighting distance, roaming radius and every camera limit are in the ARENA config at the top of retro.js. Pokémon use the space: repositioning runs stay within reach of the foe, duels drift toward parts of the field they have not used yet, they cross to the far side of the foe, sprint long distances, and physical moves start with a real run-up when the target is far away. Damage numbers and labels are world-anchored; the HUD stays in screen space.
  - *Group camera:* the base shot fits a box around every active Pokémon (sprite, name tag, shadow) into the part of the screen not covered by the HUD (and the Reels caption in 9:16), and also zooms by how far apart they are: close duels get a close camera, separation pulls it back. Dead zones on zoom and position stop it breathing while they circle; zooming out to keep everyone in frame always wins over smoothing and over the minimum-zoom limit, and the target is clamped to the world edges. Event shots (focus, wind-up, dash tracking, impact, finishing knockout) lean and push in on top of the group framing but are capped so no active Pokémon leaves the frame.
  - *Camera director:* a base framing keeps every Pokémon and projectile in view; battle events then request prioritised shots on top of it: lean toward whoever acts, a push-in on big wind-ups, tracking a dash, a punch-in on each landed hit sized by the damage actually dealt, and a cinematic finish on the deciding knockout. The highest-priority shot wins (so the camera never ping-pongs between two events), every transition is eased by a critically damped spring, shots expire back to the overview on their own, and shake is smooth rather than random per frame. Camera offsets and sprite anchors snap to whole device pixels so pixel art does not shimmer. The HUD and banners stay fixed while damage numbers move with the action.
  - *Dash strikes* (Quick Attack, Extreme Speed, Aqua Jet, Accelerock...): anticipation (crouch, lean back, eye glint), an accelerating curved dash with afterimages and the camera tracking it, contact with hit-stop and a damage-sized punch-in, the target's recoil, follow-through past the target with a skid, and a hop back to range. A miss, Protect or an immunity shows a whiff instead of an impact (for every physical move), and damage is still applied once by the battle code.
  - *Movement personalities:* from base Speed and on-screen size: fast Pokémon snap into motion, heavy ones are slow to start and stop with weighty footfalls (dust and a faint tremor), small ones take short quick hops, and flyers lift as they pick up speed.
  - *Footwork with a purpose:* movement follows the rhythm of the fight. After a contact hit a Pokémon backs off out of reach; after a ranged move it sidesteps or holds its ground. While it waits for its next move it makes one deliberate move round the foe toward fresh ground (flank, arc, or crossing to its far side), then stands and watches. About a second before acting it sets up at the range of the planned move, walking in close for a punch or bite and keeping its distance for a beam, then plants its feet. Badly hurt Pokémon retreat. New goals never make it turn on the spot, long repositioning runs become sprints with dust, and the Pokémon faces where it runs only on a real run; stepping about, it keeps its eyes on the foe (stepping back, its walk runs in reverse).
  - *Arena damage:* big hits blast craters (dark pit, raised rim, loose cobbles and pebbles, smoke). Fire leaves burning patches that become scorch marks, Water leaves puddles that splash when walked through, Ice freezes the ground, Poison leaves bubbling pools, Electric leaves crackling burns, Lava Plume leaves a lava pool that cools to rock, Earthquake rips fissures across the field, and Rock Slide / Stone Edge leave boulders. The damage builds up over the battle and resets for the next one.
  - *Stats drive the fight:* how often a Pokémon attacks and how snappy its wind-ups, lunges and charges are come from its real Speed (with stages and paralysis), so fast Pokémon act first and more often. Impact size, screen shake and stagger follow the actual damage dealt as a share of max HP. Knockback compares the attacker's Attack/Sp. Atk with the defender's Defense/Sp. Def: sturdy Pokémon dig in and barely move, frail ones get sent flying. Protect reads, switching and replacement picks use the official damage formula both ways plus who is faster.
  - *Strategy:* trainers recall a Pokémon stuck in a bad type matchup and send in a counter ("Come back!" → red recall beam → "Go!"; at most 3 switches per side, 12 s apart). Attacks in flight whiff when their target leaves. A Pokémon that knows Protect/Detect reads big or super-effective attacks and blocks them in time, and CPUs don't waste attacks into a Protect. Human players can switch with the party buttons under their moves.
  - *Defence & timing:* a short ready crouch telegraphs every attack; heavy hits and critical hits stagger the target (dizzy stars on crits) and make it back off; resisted hits flash a shield, immune Pokémon shrug; fast Pokémon side-hop out of the way as the foe winds up an attack; priority moves recover faster. Trainers shout some commands and react when their Pokémon is hit hard.
  - *Physical moves* play in three beats (wind-up, strike, follow-through with a brief impact freeze): Bite/Crunch jaws snap shut with a head-shake, Double Kick is a jump kick plus a spinning kick, tails whip through the target, Quick Attack blurs in with afterimages. Status moves have body language too (Growl/Roar sound waves, Tail Whip wag, Leer glare, Taunt anger mark, Protect dome, Hypnosis spiral).
  - *Mega Evolution:* the Key Stone shines, a rainbow helix links trainer and Pokémon, a sphere of light gathers energy and shatters, and the new form appears with the Mega emblem. It owns the battle: it waits until no attack is in motion (nobody starts a new one meanwhile), then every other Pokémon stops, turns to watch and holds its moves, status damage and the clock pause, and the fight resumes once the new form is revealed.

- **👾 Pixel sprites in battle:** 1v1, Battle Royale and Team Brawl use the same animated Mystery Dungeon pixel sprites as Adventure (see *The look* below): they walk in 8 directions and play their own attack, ranged attack, power-up, hurt, sleep and faint animations for every move, hit and knockout, sized for a duel (big Pokémon a little smaller). 32 Mega forms have their own; the other Megas wear their species' sprite. The eight Pokémon without one use a still Gen 5-style pixel sprite.
- **👑 Battle Royale & 👥 Team Brawl** (pick the format at the top of the team builder): up to 8 Pokémon on the field at once. *Battle Royale* is a free-for-all (2–8 entrants, last one standing wins); *Team Brawl* puts both whole teams on the field together (1–4 per side). Each Pokémon picks its own target (close, weakened or one it hits hard), gets a name tag and HP bar over its head, and the HUD shows a mini card per Pokémon plus a knockout feed. Intro card, sudden death, the final-blow replay and the MVP end card all work here too. To keep a busy field readable: Pokémon pair off into duels (they leave alone a Pokémon that is already under attack; in Team Brawl each one has a lane opponent) and keep away from other duels; at most two attacks play at once, and a big move (Mega Evolution, the deciding blow, a sure knockout or a 120+ power move, at most every 4 s) takes the stage alone while the camera zooms onto that duel and everyone else fades back; minor texts, move names and screen shake/flashes of ordinary hits are toned down; Team Brawl draws a team-coloured ring under each Pokémon and lock-on brackets mark every new target. Multi battles are CPU only and are not added to the records.

- **🌲 Adventure (open-world survival RPG):** explore a top-down pixel world with your partner and grow it from level 5, recruiting a team of up to three along the way. Wild Pokémon fight with the very same engine as the battles (moves, animations, official damage formula, type chart, status, AI); only the frame around it changes.
  - *The world* (`world.js`): a seeded 200 × 150-tile map (about 45 s to walk across) of connected regions, built so the danger grows away from the start: **Starting Grassland** (Lv 1–5) → **Whispering Forest** (4–10, dense trees and hidden trails) → **Riverlands** (7–15, lakes and a river with bridges) → **Echo Caves** (12–22, dark: a pool of light around you) → **Windswept Highlands** (18–30, cliff formations and snow; you get hungry faster) → **Ancient Ruins** (25–45). Paths are carved between all regions with A*, so every region, landmark and resource is reachable; the start is a clear camp. The same seed always builds the same world (a save stores the seed, not the map). Ground is painted in chunks on first sight; trees, rocks, walls and landmarks are depth-sorted with the Pokémon. A minimap with fog of war shows what you have explored.
  - *The look*: Pokémon are animated pixel sprites in the three-quarter view of the Mystery Dungeon games (`pixelmon.js`, from the [PMD Sprite Collab](https://github.com/PMDCollab/SpriteCollab)): they walk in 8 directions and have their own idle, attack, ranged attack, power-up, hurt, sleep and faint animations, which the battle engine plays for every move, hit and knockout; one sprite pixel is one pixel of the world, so they sit in it like the trees do. 473 of the 481 Pokémon have one (Falinks, Zarude, Gimmighoul, Miraidon, Wo-Chien, Chien-Pao, Ting-Lu and Pecharunt use a still Gen 5-style pixel sprite). 32 Mega forms have their own sprite too; the other Megas wear their species' sprite. **The sprites are CC BY-NC 4.0: non-commercial use only, with credit to their artists (`pmd/CREDITS.md`).**
  - *Wild Pokémon*: spawn just out of sight, never near a camp, on ground you can reach, at most a few at a time; they despawn when left far behind. Each biome has its own encounter table (rare finds included); the level comes from the region's danger within the biome's range, pulled a little toward your level, with random spread, and a Pokémon evolves to the stage its level allows. About 1 in 20 is an **Alpha** (bigger, stronger, more HP, better loot). Each landmark is guarded by a **boss** (beaten bosses stay beaten). Temperament comes from the species: aggressive ones first **notice** you (a "?" with a filling meter and a yellow ring: back off and they lose interest), then turn hostile ("!", a red ring, a sound) and give you a moment before their first attack; a pulsing red ring warns just before every attack, and they never attack from off screen (arrows at the screen edge point to hostile or watchful ones out of view). One far weaker than you leaves you alone; one far stronger is territorial (it only reacts up close and gives up sooner) and its level shows in red, with a DANGER notice when it first appears. Neutral ones only fight back, timid ones run when they are losing; all give up when you get away or retreat to camp.
  - *Your Pokémon*: experience on the official "medium fast" curve and the Gen 5 scaled formula (a foe above your level is worth more, one far below it much less, so the next biome becomes the better place to grow), stats from the official formulas on every level-up, new moves from its real level-up learnset (four equipped, swap them with **M**), level evolutions (Charmander → Charmeleon at 16). Moves cost **stamina** (from their power and PP) instead of PP; stamina also powers the dash. **Hunger** drops over time: low hunger slows stamina, starving drains HP down to a quarter (it can't kill). Out of combat and fed, HP slowly returns.
  - *The team*: a beaten wild Pokémon sometimes asks to join you (Alphas and rare ones more often; the first one sooner). Up to three: the ones waiting share half of the XP (they level up, learn and evolve on their own), **F** or a click / tap on a picture under your card sends one out, and when the one out faints the next one jumps in; you only wake up at camp when the whole team is down. Resting heals everyone. The **T** panel shows the team (send out, release, take held items).
  - *Fighting*: your target (where **1–4** go) is marked by corner brackets around its body and a framed tag with a pointer, drawn over everything (yellow, red once it is hostile), with the other tags dimmed, and shown in a **target card** at the top (picture, level in danger colours, types, mood, HP). The move buttons show how well each move works on it (×2, ×½, ✕). A Pokémon you pick (**Q**, click, tap, or TARGET on touch) stays picked while it is on screen; otherwise the target is the nearest one in range, and a foe that turns on you takes over from a calm one. **Dodge**: in a fight, a tap of **Shift / Space** (DODGE on touch) is a quick burst that attacks pass by (a little stamina back for a good one).
  - *Day, night and weather*: a day takes 10 minutes of play. At night the world turns blue around your light, Ghost, Dark and Poison types come out (with night-only Pokémon such as Hoothoot) and Dark and Ghost moves hit harder. Rain (grass, forest, river) brings Water types and powers Water moves (Fire is weaker); snow in the Highlands brings Ice types.
  - *Dungeons*: beat a landmark's guardian and the way into the depths below it opens (**E** there): three floors in the dark, each somewhere else in the biome, with hostile Pokémon about and the stairs to find (an arrow points to them); a Depth Guardian waits on the bottom floor, then treasure. Fainting with no one left ends the dive. **Today's Alpha**: each real day one strong Alpha roams one biome (the same for everyone), with a big reward.
  - *Money and the shop*: Poké Dollars (₽) from fights, caches, quests and dungeons. Trader Milo sells supplies, **held items** (Muscle Band, Wise Glasses, Quick Claw, Leftovers, Lucky Egg: one per Pokémon, given from the bag) and **TMs** (moves of its type, its family's moves and the game's TM moves, taught to the Pokémon that is out), and buys materials.
  - *Pokédex* (**X**): each biome's Pokémon, seen and beaten (silhouettes until seen; rare, night-only and rain-only ones marked); beating every regular Pokémon of a biome pays out once.
  - *Finding the way*: a blue arrow at the screen edge (a marker once it is on screen, and a blinking dot on the minimap) points to the next goal: the stairs, a quest's landmark, biome or resource, or the Ranger when a quest is ready. A new adventure starts with six short tips (move, talk, target and attack, dodge, eat and rest) that each wait until you have done it; **Skip tips** hides them.
  - *Gathering, items, crafting*: berry bushes, herbs, mushrooms, apple trees, sticks, stones, ore and crystals regrow after a while; hidden caches hold supplies. Berries heal or cure (Oran, Sitrus, Pecha, Rawst, Cheri, Leppa), food fills you up; craft Berry Juice, Forest Stew, Energy Tonic, Max Elixir and a **Campfire Kit** (a new rest-and-save spot).
  - *People* (drawn with the pixel-trainer art): **Ranger Ivy** at Base Camp hands out quests and pays the rewards when you report back; **Hiker Bram** in the forest has a welcome gift and tips; **Trader Milo** in the Riverlands swaps materials for supplies and keeps a campfire (a second rest-and-save spot); **Scholar Wren** in the Highlands studies Old Relics and Glow Crystals for XP. Talking pauses the world; answer with **1–6** or a tap.
  - *Quests and places*: take up to three quests at a time from the Ranger (forage, defeat, craft, discover a landmark, reach a level, beat a boss), finish them, then report for XP and items; discovering landmarks and new biomes gives XP.
  - *Rest and saving*: rest at the camp (or a campsite) with **E** to heal fully and save; the game also autosaves every 45 s out of combat and when you leave. Fainting wakes you at your last rest spot. Saves are validated when loaded (anything missing or invalid falls back to a safe value).
  - *Controls*: **WASD/arrows** move, **Shift/Space** run (a tap in a fight: dodge), **1–4** attack your target (**Q** or a click / tap on one picks another), **E** interact / talk, **5–7** quick items, **F** switch Pokémon, **I** bag & crafting, **M** moves & stats, **T** team, **X** Pokédex, **J** quests & places, **Esc** pause. *On a touch screen*: a floating stick that appears where your left thumb lands, the four moves as big round buttons in an arc around **E** (with cooldown sweeps), a **RUN** toggle, **DODGE** and **TARGET** buttons, BAG / MOVES / TEAM / QUESTS / DEX buttons, vibration on taps and hits, and the screen edges flash red when you are hit. The layout follows the phone: portrait when it is held upright (the camera pulls back a little so the narrow screen still shows enough around you), landscape when it is turned sideways, and turning it mid-game just switches layouts. You can only target what is on screen. The camera eases into its look-ahead and fight framing.
  - All balancing (speeds, ranges, XP, stamina, hunger, spawn rules, level formula, encounter tables, items, recipes, quests, people and their trades) is in the tables at the top of `adventure.js`.

- **⭐ Journey: The Eclipse of Valoria (a trainer's adventure):** you are the trainer. Walk a hand-made region of towns, routes, forests and houses, catch wild Pokémon in the tall grass, battle trainers and gym leaders, collect badges and follow the story. Battles use the same animated engine as the arena. Your lead Pokémon (the first one in the party that can still battle) walks behind you with its animated sprite, indoors and out; reorder the party to change who walks with you, and press A facing it to see how it feels (it depends on its friendship, HP and status).
  - *The story so far (Chapter 1)*: wake up in **Willowbrook Town**, get the Running Shoes from Mom, choose a partner at **Professor Hazel**'s lab (any region's starter trio, Gen 1–9) and battle your rival **Sora**. On **Route 1** an old man teaches catching and the first trainers wait. **Verdant Town** has a Pokémon Center, a Poké Mart and the Grass gym, but its leader **Luna** has gone to **Whisperwood Forest**, where **Team Eclipse** is digging up a Meteor Shard. Beat them, then win the **Leaf Badge**, a TM and the **Ride Pager** (Scyther: Cut). The astronomer Orrin starts the 12-shard side quest.
  - *Chapter 2: Tides of Port Coralis*: with the Leaf Badge the rockslide is cleared and **Route 2** opens east of Verdant Town (four trainers, a pond, a ledge, a Meteor Shard in the meadow, and a second battle with Sora). In **Port Coralis** the gym is locked: its leader **Rex** is at the **Maritime Museum**, where Team Eclipse has stolen the Coral Meteorite. Chase them into **Seacliff Cave** (wild Pokémon on the cave floor, two grunts, a hidden shard) and beat **Admin Kael**, bring the meteorite home, then win the **Tide Badge**, TM Water Pulse and the **Exp. Share** (Pokémon that sit a battle out get half its Exp. Points). Chapter 3 (Voltara City) is next. Saves from before Chapter 2 that already have the Leaf Badge find Route 2 open.
  - *Catching and battling*: in a battle you pick moves (**1–4**), switch, and use the command bar: **BAG** (medicine; the battle pauses while you choose), **BALL** (official Gen 3/4 catch formula: catch rate, HP, status, ball) and **RUN** (wild battles). HP, status and PP carry over between battles. In the **POKéMON** menu, drag a Pokémon up or down (by its grip on a touch screen) to change the order; the first one goes out first. XP uses each species' growth curve, shared by the Pokémon that fought; level-ups teach moves (forget one when four are known), and evolution happens by level, stone, friendship or the Link Stone (cancel it with **B / Esc**). Losing with no Pokémon left sends you to the last Pokémon Center with half your money.
  - *The world*: trainers spot you and walk up for a battle; tall grass hides wild Pokémon (Repel keeps weak ones away); ledges can only be jumped down; items lie on the ground and hidden ones sparkle; doors, mats and stairs lead in and out. Party of 6, PC boxes at Pokémon Centers, a Bag with pockets, a 481-entry Pokédex (seen / caught), a trainer card with 7 badge slots.
  - *Controls*: **WASD/arrows** walk, **Shift** (or **X**) run, **E / Space / Enter** talk, read and advance text, **Esc / M** menu (Pokémon, Pokédex, Bag, trainer card, save). The menus work from the keyboard too: **arrows** move the cursor, **Enter / Space / Z** pick, **Esc / X** go back one screen, **M** closes; in the Bag **← →** turn the pockets. The battle BAG works the same way (and **1–9** pick a row). On a phone: a stick, **A**, **B** (run) and **MENU**.
  - *Unlimited money & items*: Settings → **💰 Journey: unlimited money & items** keeps your money at ¥999,999 and every item you can use or spend at 99 (Rare Candies, all evolution stones, every Poké Ball including the Master Ball, all medicine, Repels), and gives you every TM; the Bag shows ∞. Key items are left alone (the story counts some of them). Turning it off keeps what you have. Journey only.
  - The game saves when you change maps, after battles and from the menu (its own save slot). Files: `journey.js` (the engine and menus), `jmaps.js` (the maps), `jstory.js` (items, trainers, all dialog and events), `jart.js` (the pixel art), `progress.js` (growth curves, XP, evolution, catch odds).

## Run it
Double-click **`start.bat`**. It starts a tiny local server and opens http://localhost:8080 in Chrome/Edge.
The game must run through that server (it loads its scripts as modules and fetches the sprite sheets); opening `index.html` directly shows a reminder.

Retro 2D draws its canvas at CSS resolution at most (max 1920 px wide) and lets the browser upscale it with hard pixel edges, packs all particle discs on shared atlas sheets, caches tinted sprite frames (afterimages, frozen / poisoned tints), and shrinks its particle budget for a while if frames start running long, so it holds 60 fps on ordinary laptops (an 8-Pokémon Battle Royale measured 59.6-59.9 fps at 1920x1080, 125% scaling, on Intel Iris Xe).

A live frame-rate readout sits at the top of the screen in every mode (arena, tournaments, Adventure, Journey): frames per second, the average frame time, and the low (the slowest frame of the last half second, so a stutter shows even when the average looks fine), green / yellow / red at 55+ / 30+ / below 30. **F3** or Settings → **Show FPS** turns it on and off; it is not part of the canvas, so it never shows up in recordings.

## Play
1. **Team builder:** browse the Pokédex by generation tab (All, Gen 1–9) or search, then click Pokémon to add them to the highlighted player. On a phone the settings fold behind **⚙ Settings**; held sideways, the Pokédex and the teams sit side by side with one row of buttons below. Set level, Mega Stone, 4 moves, or evolve/de-evolve each slot.
   Evolved forms can't go below their real evolution level (Charizard ≥ 36). Pick Human or CPU for each player.
2. **Battle:** choose a move, or switch. If your Pokémon holds a Mega Stone, toggle **Mega Evolve** before picking a move (once per battle).
3. **Auto-play:** tick "Auto-play next random battle" for endless CPU vs CPU battles.
4. **Journey:** click **⭐ Journey** in the team builder, enter a name and pick a look, or **Continue**. A new game opens with a short pixel-art film (`jintro.js`), shot like a movie (letterbox bars with the narration as subtitles, a camera that pushes in, tracks and punches on impacts, slow motion, light rays and shockwaves): the meteor that shattered over Valoria, its shards becoming Mega Stones and Key Stones, a Mega Evolution, Rayquaza over the crater, Team Eclipse under the eclipse, then Professor Hazel's welcome, with its own music. It plays by itself; a tap / Space hurries it along and **Skip** / Esc ends it. **🎬 Watch the intro** on the title screen plays it again.
5. **Adventure:** click **🌲 Adventure** in the team builder, pick a partner (and optionally a world seed), or **Continue** your saved adventure.

## Record for upload
Click **⏺ Record** (or press **R**), then pick **this tab** and tick "share tab audio" for the cries.
Every battle is saved as its own video (`pokemon-battle-<date>.mp4`) in your Downloads folder. Press R again to stop.
Combine with auto-play + Turbo speed to record lots of battles hands-free.

## Add more Pokémon
Add an evolution chain's first species to `ROOTS` in `tools/build-data.mjs`, then run:
```
node tools/build-data.mjs
node tools/build-pmd.mjs
```
Data and cries come from PokéAPI (https://pokeapi.co); the sprites and pictures come from build-pmd.mjs.

## Rebuild the pixel sprites
`node tools/build-pmd.mjs` downloads the pixel sprites (Adventure and battles) into `pmd/` (about 54 MB, Mega forms included; Pokémon already downloaded are skipped) and writes `pmd/index.json` and `pmd/CREDITS.md`. It also makes `faces/`, the pixel picture of every Pokémon and Mega form that the menus and HUDs show: its sprite's first standing frame facing the viewer, at twice its size. A Mega or Pokémon with no sprite of its own uses the Gen 5-style sprite from [Pokémon Showdown](https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/sprites/gen5/) (Smogon's sprite project), or else its species' picture.

`node tools/test-journey.mjs` checks Journey's maps and story without a browser: every map is well-formed and linked both ways, nothing stands inside a wall, and playing the story's flags forward from a new game reaches every map, person and item and every chapter goal (so the story can't get stuck).

`node tools/test-play.mjs` plays Journey for real in Chrome or Edge (headless; `--show` to watch): a bot uses the game's own controls (arrow keys, Enter, number keys, the starter picker, the shop and PC, the move buttons, BALL / RUN / BAG) and plays every chapter from a new game: every person, item, trainer, gym and story event, plus the menus, a reload and Continue, and a whiteout. It fails the moment the game gets stuck (nothing on screen changes for 30 s), on any JavaScript error or missing file, or when a story step doesn't happen, and then prints what was on screen, the last lines of dialog and a screenshot. A full run takes about 25 minutes; `--from=extras` or `--from=story2` starts from a ready-made end-of-Chapter-1 save, `--chapter=1` stops after Chapter 1, and `--seed=N` repeats a run (the starter and the bot's choices). To keep runs short it raises the party's level before the bigger battles (the story is what is tested, not the grind).
`node tools/make-trailer.mjs` makes a trailer for Journey from the game itself and saves it as `videos/journey-trailer.mp4` (1280x720, about 2:20, with sound): the opening film's big moments with title cards, then real gameplay (choosing a partner, exploring, catching, a gym battle, evolving through the Bag, story scenes, a Mega Evolution battle against the Champion, a legendary, the menus) and an end card, over its own score. A director script inside the page stages each shot, and the browser records its own tab (no ffmpeg needed). The browser writes a streaming MP4 (no length, no index, so players cannot skip through it); `tools/remux-mp4.mjs` then rewrites it as an ordinary MP4 with a length, a seek index and a keyframe every second, so it plays and scrubs in any player and can be sent anywhere. It copies the video and sound as they are (no re-encoding); run it by hand on any such recording with `node tools/remux-mp4.mjs <file.mp4>`. `--webm` records WebM instead, `--size=1920x1080` a bigger picture, `--show` opens a visible window.

`node tools/test-world.mjs [seeds]` checks the Adventure world generator over many random seeds (same seed = same world, a clear start, every biome present, every region, landmark and resource reachable).

The Retro 2D font is Tiny5 (SIL Open Font License, Google Fonts) in `lib/fonts/`.

## Not implemented (yet)
Abilities, held items other than Mega Stones, weather/terrain, and a few rare moves (Sky Attack, Dive, Phantom Force, Explosion…). Moves the engine can't run are hidden from move lists.
